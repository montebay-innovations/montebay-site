Case study

Svelio

iOS · Android · live

A nutrition app that reads food labels and analyzes meal photos. It started as a Next.js web app. To become a real mobile product it needed things a website cannot do, and it had to pass Apple and Google review. We designed, built and launched it end to end.

App Store Google Play
What we built

One codebase, three platforms

Native shells for a web app

The Next.js app runs inside native iOS and Android shells with Capacitor, extended by custom plugins in Swift and Kotlin for health data, home-screen widgets, push notifications and LiDAR plate measurement.

Subscriptions everywhere

In-app purchases through StoreKit and Google Play Billing, managed with RevenueCat, plus Stripe on the web. One entitlement system, and restore purchases that work.

Two-way health data

Meals sync to Apple Health and Android Health Connect, and activity data flows back into the app.

AI features

Meal-photo analysis with Claude and Gemini vision models, matched against USDA nutrition data, and nutrition-label reading with AWS Textract.

A production backend

AWS Lambda, DynamoDB and S3 behind a Vercel deployment, with error monitoring, separate staging and production environments, and more than 3,000 automated tests.

Finish

Light and dark themes, a full-screen iPad layout, and offline support that queues actions until the connection comes back.

App Review

Getting through Apple and Google

Apple's first review flagged subscription disclosure requirements, guidelines 3.1.2(c) and 2.1(b), and surfaced a restore-purchase bug. We fixed the paywall disclosures and the restore flow, replied to App Review, resubmitted, and Svelio was approved in May 2026. On Android it went through Google Play closed testing with the Play billing chain verified end to end before launching publicly.
